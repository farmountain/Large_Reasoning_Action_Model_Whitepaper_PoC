We present the Large Reasoning and Action Model (LRAM), a hierarchical architecture for machine-assisted mathematical discovery and validated action selection. LRAM combines three complementary intelligences: a Prajna abstraction layer that performs conceptual compression of a problem into a bounded candidate space, a Grover-style quantum amplitude amplification layer that accelerates search over that space, and a three-tier validation layer that integrates formal verification, causal identification with do-interventions, and circuit-level mechanistic probes. LRAM is trained as a student under a multi-teacher distillation regime drawing from large language models, large action models, and task-specialised small models, with a Reflexion-enhanced self-critique loop closing the outer training circuit. We state the architecture in first-principles mathematical form, derive its convergence properties under a bounded-oracle assumption, and demonstrate a complete open-source proof-of-concept and proof-of-value workshop that can be executed end-to-end. The ambitious target of the programme is the Clay Mathematics Institute Millennium Prize Problems, approached through a staged roadmap that respects oracle-hardness and undecidability boundaries. The defensible claim of the whitepaper is that LRAM is a convergence accelerator for formal mathematical discovery with a credible path toward Millennium-adjacent fragments, not a universal solver.
